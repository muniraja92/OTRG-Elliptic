## Name decision

The working name TRUST-GRAF ("Trustworthy Graph-Augmented Adaptive Fusion") is not justified by the evidence. The fusion turned out not to be adaptive in practice, because the gate is nearly constant, and "trustworthy" implies guarantees the study does not provide. GRAF also collides with the user's GRAFT papers. The manuscript therefore uses the descriptive name OTRG (out-of-time reliability gating). The code keeps the label TRUST-GRAF so that every result file remains traceable; the README states the mapping. An exhaustive acronym search for OTRG has not been done and should precede submission.

## Answers to the adversarial audit questions

The method is distinguishable from Mowst and feature-weighted stacking only by its out-of-time fitting protocol and drift-sensitive gate inputs, and the experiments show that neither distinction produces a measurable benefit. The research gap is real: whether graph information can be used selectively under the Elliptic shift. But the answer obtained is negative. The mathematical components are implemented as written. The data are real, sourced and checksummed, and the splits are leakage-safe by construction, because no edge crosses a time step. Baselines are fair and include the strongest feasible tree ensembles. The GCN is weaker than in its original paper's protocol and has only three seeds, which is disclosed. Metrics were recomputed independently. Ablations isolate the gate's inputs, its adaptivity and the graph view. Statistical comparisons use transaction-level stratified bootstrap and step-level Wilcoxon tests with Holm correction, but ignore dependence within subgraphs. The conclusions in the manuscript are negative or qualified and match the evidence. Mixed and negative results are reported prominently, including in the abstract. Every run can be reproduced within the stated budget from the README commands.

## Publication-readiness gate

Gate 1 (novelty): FAIL for a methods contribution. The gate is a two-expert, meta-feature stacking scheme with a new fitting protocol, and the protocol gives no benefit over a constant gate.

Gate 2 (methodological correctness): PASS. Implementation, equations and evaluation are consistent, and the automated and manual checks found no leakage.

Gate 3 (experimental evidence): FAIL for the original claim that reliability gating improves detection or robustness under shift. The evidence does support two narrower claims: the propagated graph view carries a small real signal relative to a shuffle control, and per-transaction gating gives no benefit.

Gate 4 (generalisation and reliability): FAIL at present. There is one data set, every method collapses after the closure, the models are fragile to mild feature noise, and the bootstrap ignores within-subgraph dependence.

Gate 5 (reproducibility): CONDITIONAL PASS. Code, configuration, checksums, predictions and models are complete and deterministic. The data licence must be confirmed on the Kaggle page, and a public code repository URL is needed for a code-availability statement stronger than "on request".

Overall decision: FAIL as a methodological Q1 submission. CONDITIONAL PASS as a carefully controlled negative-result or measurement article, provided the following are added: at least one independent second data set (for example Elliptic2 or an IBM synthetic AML set, clearly labelled as synthetic); five-seed GCN and GraphSAGE baselines at the original learning rate; a cluster bootstrap over connected components; nested temporal tuning; an investigation of the feature-noise fragility (per-feature noise scaled by robust spread rather than standard deviation); and the licence and repository items above.

## Recommended journal category and next steps

The honest framing fits venues that publish rigorous empirical or negative results in applied machine learning, or the reproducibility tracks of data-mining venues, rather than methods-focused Q1 journals. Specific journal scope and indexing should be verified before choosing. The most promising scientific direction suggested by the data is not view selection but shift detection: the gate's novelty signal did not move across the closure, so a dedicated study of whether any label-free signal anticipates the post-closure collapse would address the real bottleneck.

## Independent review and the corrections it triggered

The independent reviewer (`independent_review.md`) recommended major revision, bordering on rejection as a methods paper, and found no test-set leakage. The following points were verified and corrected in the manuscript. The abstract mixed metrics when describing the graph signal; it now reports the real-versus-rewired comparison on both metrics and states that the PR-AUC gain over the node-only model is not significant. The rewired view alone raises F1 over the node-only model, and this is now stated. The edge-shuffle control rewires endpoints uniformly within a step and preserves only per-step edge counts, not degrees; the Discussion's description was wrong and has been corrected. The GCN used three seeds, not five, as the methods text had stated. The node-only model is unchanged by edge removal by construction, and the claim that the gated model was "least affected" was removed. The feature-noise test is mis-scaled for these heavy-tailed features (27 features with zero IQR); it is now reported as such and listed for a rerun with robust scaling. The post-hoc nature of the penalty revision (the first-run log printed the mean test-period gate weight, a label-free quantity), the dual use of steps 30 to 34 for tuning and calibration, the bootstrap p-value floor, per-metric rather than cross-metric correction, and the missing GraphSAGE, GAT, Label-GCN and Mowst baselines are now disclosed in the Limitations. The reviewer's remaining requests (a degree-preserving configuration-model shuffle, a cluster bootstrap with seed-level tests, a full GNN baseline set, larger tuning grids, a second data set) need additional compute and are left open; they do not change the readiness decision above.
