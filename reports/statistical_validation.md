# Statistical validation (Agent 7 role)

Sources: `results/metrics.csv` (per seed), `results/metrics_summary.csv`, `results/statistical_results.csv` (primary family), `results/statistical_results_secondary.csv` (graph-view family), `results/per_timestep_f1.csv`, `results/sensitivity_summary.csv`, `results/gate_summary.csv`. All are produced by `experiments/statistical_tests.py`.

## Design

The unit of analysis for the bootstrap is the test transaction, resampled within its time step so that the composition of the test period across steps is preserved. Each bootstrap replicate recomputes the metric for every seed of both methods and compares the seed-averaged values, so seeds are treated as repeated fits on the same test cases rather than as independent samples. Training seeds affect only bagging and column subsampling in the tree models and initialisation in the GCN; they do not resample the data, so seed-to-seed standard deviations describe optimisation variability, not sampling uncertainty, and are reported separately from the bootstrap confidence intervals. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test uses the 15 test time steps as paired units on seed-averaged per-step F1. It respects the temporal structure but has low power, and steps with very few illicit transactions contribute noisy F1 values.

Two families are corrected separately with Holm's procedure: the primary family compares the proposed method with each of the other methods, and the secondary family contains the pre-planned graph-view comparisons (graph-augmented against node-only, graph-augmented against shuffled graph, shuffled graph against node-only) together with reference comparisons of the strongest baselines. Effect sizes are reported as raw metric differences with 95% percentile intervals, which are directly interpretable for PR-AUC and F1; the rank-biserial correlation is reported for the Wilcoxon test. McNemar's test was not used, because thresholds differ between methods and seeds and the primary quantities are threshold-free (PR-AUC) or seed-averaged.

## Findings

The proposed method's differences from equal averaging, logistic stacking, the constant gate and the structural-only gate are all within ±0.001 in PR-AUC and ±0.007 in F1. Against equal averaging, logistic stacking and the constant gate, none is significant after correction in either test. The structural-only gate is the exception: its F1 is lower by 0.006, which is significant in the bootstrap (Holm-adjusted p = 0.013) but not in the step-level Wilcoxon test, so the disagreement and entropy inputs contribute a small thresholded-detection effect that the constant gate also reproduces. Against the node-only LightGBM, the PR-AUC difference is +0.0010 with a bootstrap interval that excludes zero before correction but a Holm-adjusted p of about 0.10, so no ranking improvement is claimed; the F1 difference of +0.010 is significant (Holm-adjusted p = 0.013), and it comes from mixing in the graph-augmented expert, which on its own has a higher F1 than the proposed method. The tuned random forest has the highest F1 and the proposed method has a significantly lower F1 than it; the proposed method has a significantly higher PR-AUC than the random forest. The two methods therefore trade ranking quality against thresholded detection, and neither dominates.

In the secondary family, the real neighbourhood view raises F1 over the node-only model and raises PR-AUC over the shuffled-graph model, while the shuffled view lowers PR-AUC relative to the node-only model. However, the rewired view also raises F1 over the node-only model (+0.012), so only the real-versus-rewired difference (+0.0054 PR-AUC, +0.0072 F1) can be attributed to the actual topology, and the PR-AUC gain of the real view over the node-only model (+0.0004) is not significant. This is the only result in the study that supports a positive claim, and it concerns the information content of the graph, not the gate. With B = 1,000 the smallest attainable Holm-adjusted p is about 0.014 in the primary family and 0.007 in the secondary family, and many reported p-values sit at that floor.

## Assumptions and caveats

The bootstrap treats test transactions within a step as exchangeable, which ignores dependence between transactions connected in the same subgraph; intervals may therefore be somewhat narrow. A cluster bootstrap over connected components would be more conservative and is listed as pending. Post-closure F1 rests on 169 illicit transactions over seven steps and is not used for any significance claim. The gate penalty was selected once on validation data after the first run; sensitivity over the full grid shows test PR-AUC varying in the fourth decimal place only.
