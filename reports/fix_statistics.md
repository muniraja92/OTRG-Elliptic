# Fix report: statistical validation (revision v2), 23 Nov 2026

## Scope and what was done

This report answers the five statistical criticisms in `reports/independent_review.md`. (a) The v1 bootstrap resampled single transactions and ignored graph dependence. (b) Training seeds were never treated as a unit of variation. (c) With B = 1000 the p-values sat at a floor. (d) Holm correction was applied per metric only. (e) The 15-step Wilcoxon test has low power. Everything was implemented in a new script, `experiments/statistical_tests_v2.py`. `experiments/statistical_tests.py` was not modified. The new script finds every method in `results/preds` automatically. It applies exactly the v1 calibration, imported from `src.evaluation`: for each method and seed, a Platt map and the F1-optimal threshold are fitted on the labelled out-of-time predictions of steps 30 to 34, then applied once to the labelled test nodes of steps 35 to 49. Following the specification, test metrics were looked at while the code was debugged. This involves no model choice. The only analysis choice, the primary resampling scheme described below, was fixed before any v2 p-value was computed.

The final run was started at the end of the 90-minute waiting window: `flock /tmp/cpu.lock python3 experiments/statistical_tests_v2.py > logs/statistical_v2_final.log`. It took 114 s with a peak RSS of 1.51 GB. It produced `results/statistical_v2_primary.csv`, `results/statistical_v2_key.csv`, `results/statistical_v2_tost.csv`, `results/statistical_v2_seed.csv` and `results/statistical_v2_components.csv`. It found 19 methods: five seeds each for every LightGBM, RF, XGB, ensemble and ablation variant and for the GCN, three seeds each for GraphSAGE and Mowst, and one seed for LR. Seeds 123 and 2024 of GraphSAGE (and possibly of Mowst) were still training when the window closed (`run_gnn_v2.py sage 123 2024`). The rows involving GraphSAGE and Mowst in `statistical_v2_primary.csv` therefore rest on three seeds. No key comparison involves those methods. Re-running the same command (about 2 minutes) after those files land updates only those rows. The key, TOST and seed tables cannot change, because the random number streams are fixed (seed 20261123).

## (a) Cluster bootstrap: the specified clusters are degenerate

The specification asked for weakly connected components of the test-period transaction graph as clusters, resampled within each time step. I computed the components with `scipy.sparse.csgraph.connected_components` on all 203,769 nodes and 234,355 edges. The graph has exactly 49 weakly connected components, one per time step: every time step of Elliptic is a single connected component (`statistical_v2_components.csv`, column `n_wcc` = 1 for every test step). Resampling components within a step therefore always reproduces the same test set and gives zero variance, so the scheme as specified cannot be carried out. I did not substitute something silently. The script implements, and all tables report, three paired schemes. All three use identical replicate weights for every method.

1. `txn` repeats the v1 transaction-within-step bootstrap with B = 5000, for reference only.
2. `comm` is the primary scheme. Clusters are Louvain communities (networkx 3.7, seed 12345, resolution 1) of each undirected step graph, built on all nodes, labelled and unlabelled, and resampled with replacement within each step. The 15 test steps contain 875 communities, 701 of which hold at least one labelled node. A community has at most 522 nodes and at most 298 labelled nodes. Between 5.0% and 12.2% of the edges in a step cross community boundaries.
3. `step` uses the real weakly connected components, i.e. the 15 test steps, as clusters and resamples them with replacement without stratification. It serves as the conservative sensitivity check.

The communities are an approximation to independence, not a guarantee: about one edge in ten still links two clusters, and the two-hop neighbourhood view crosses those edges. That limitation holds whichever partition is used, because the true components are entire time steps. Only labelled test nodes (16,670, of which 1,083 illicit) enter the metrics. Each bootstrap replicate is a vector of per-node multiplicities. AP is computed as sklearn's `average_precision_score` with `sample_weight`, with ties grouped. A segment-sum formulation gives all replicates in one sparse product. The script asserts agreement with sklearn to 1e-9, and the result was checked against an earlier direct-cumsum implementation, which matched to 1e-15 on every key-table entry. A synthetic-data unit test is described in the commands section.

The cluster bootstrap widens the standard errors only moderately for the close comparisons, because paired differences between closely related LightGBM variants vary together across clusters. For TRUST-GRAF (OTRG) minus LGBM-AF, the PR-AUC standard error rises from 0.00040 (txn) to 0.00045 (comm), and the F1 standard error from 0.00184 to 0.00215. For OTRG minus LGBM-AF+G they rise from 0.00113 to 0.00143 (PR-AUC) and from 0.00213 to 0.00258 (F1). The inflation is much larger for heterogeneous pairs. For OTRG versus GCN the PR-AUC standard error is 0.0111 (txn), 0.0231 (comm) and 0.0430 (step). Taken alone, the cluster bootstrap changes few conclusions.

## (b) Seeds as a unit

`statistical_v2_seed.csv` lists, for every key comparison, the five per-seed paired differences (seeds 42, 1, 7, 123 and 2024, paired by seed value), their mean and s.d., the sign counts, an exact sign test and an exact Wilcoxon test. With n = 5, the smallest attainable two-sided p of either test is 2/2^5 = 0.0625. **A seed-level test can therefore never reach 0.05 with five seeds**, so these tests are reported as descriptive evidence of consistency. The key table also gives a two-stage bootstrap, `*_2stage`. Each replicate resamples communities within steps (as in `comm`) and, independently, the five seeds with replacement (paired by seed value), and the seed-averaged metric difference is computed from the drawn seeds. This bootstrap is itself crude, because it estimates between-seed variance from five values. Its intervals are still the only ones here that include training variance.

Seed variance is the dominant source of uncertainty for F1, which is a thresholded metric. For OTRG minus LGBM-AF, the per-seed F1 differences have mean +0.00957 and s.d. 0.00963, and 4 of 5 are positive (exact Wilcoxon p = 0.125). The two-stage F1 standard error is 0.00467, against 0.00215 under `comm` alone. For PR-AUC the seed effect is small. The same comparison has a per-seed mean of +0.00098 with s.d. 0.00024, positive in 5 of 5 seeds, which is the most consistent the test can show (p = 0.0625). The two-stage PR-AUC standard error is 0.00047, against 0.00045.

## (c) and (d) Resolution and multiplicity

All p-values are two-sided CI-inversion bootstrap p-values, p = min(1, 2(min(#{d ≤ 0}, #{d ≥ 0}) + 1)/(B + 1)). B is 5000 for the nine pre-specified key comparisons and 2000 elsewhere, so the smallest raw p is 0.0004 or 0.0010. The key family is nine comparisons crossed with two metrics, 18 tests. Holm correction over that whole family (`*_holm_family`) is reported next to per-metric Holm (`*_holm_permetric`) for every scheme. A third variant adds the step-level Wilcoxon test as a third metric (`*_holm_family3`, 27 tests). The smallest attainable family-Holm p is 18 × 2/5001 = 0.0072. Several F1 entries sit exactly at that value and should be reported as bounds (p ≤ 0.0072), not as point values. In the all-comparisons table (`statistical_v2_primary.csv`), per-metric Holm is kept within the v1 primary family (OTRG versus every other method) and the v1 secondary family (baseline pairs). A cross-metric Holm on the `comm` scheme is added as `*_holm_xmetric`.

## (e) Step-level Wilcoxon

The 15-step Wilcoxon test is retained in every table, but it is not used as the confirmatory test. After family-wide Holm (`p_wilcoxon_holm_family3`) no key comparison is significant: the smallest value is 0.5585, for LGBM-AF+G versus LGBM-AF, whose raw p is 0.0329. The low power is structural, not a resolution floor. With 15 steps the smallest attainable p is far below 0.05. But after step 43 almost no illicit transactions are labelled (2 to 24 per step in steps 43 to 47), so per-step F1 differences there are dominated by noise.

## Old versus new conclusions

**OTRG versus LGBM-AF (the node-only expert).** In v1, the PR-AUC gain of +0.0010 had raw p = 0.013 (Holm 0.104), and the F1 gain of +0.0096 was "significant" at the Holm floor of 0.014. Under the cluster bootstrap the PR-AUC gain has 95% CI [+0.0001, +0.0019], raw p = 0.0224 and family Holm 0.224, so it is not significant after correction. It was positive in all five seeds, but the effect is tiny. The F1 gain survives the cluster bootstrap: CI [+0.0058, +0.0142], p = 0.0004, family Holm ≤ 0.0072. It does not survive training-seed variance. The two-stage CI is [+0.0012, +0.0196], with raw p = 0.0248 and family Holm 0.347. Only 4 of 5 seeds are positive (exact Wilcoxon p = 0.125). **The claim that OTRG significantly beats the node-only expert should be withdrawn.** At most it is "a positive F1 difference that is not robust to seed variance".

**OTRG versus LGBM-AF+G.** The F1 deficit of −0.0094 is significant under `comm` (family Holm ≤ 0.0072). Under the two-stage bootstrap it is not: CI [−0.0220, +0.0011], p = 0.084, family Holm 0.844. Only 2 of 5 seeds are negative, and the mean per-seed difference is −0.0094 with s.d. 0.0124. The PR-AUC difference of +0.0006 is null under every scheme (two-stage p = 0.75).

**OTRG versus the constant gate, stacking and equal averaging (the negative result).** None of the nine p-values is below 0.2 under any scheme. TOST with margins of ±0.005 PR-AUC and ±0.01 F1 (`statistical_v2_tost.csv`) establishes equivalence for all three comparisons on both metrics under `comm` and `step`. The p_TOST of 0.0002 is the B = 5000 floor (0.0012 after Holm over the six tests of each scheme). Under the two-stage bootstrap, PR-AUC equivalence still holds for all three, with 90% CIs inside ±0.001. F1 equivalence holds for A-static-gate (90% CI [−0.0062, +0.0068], p_TOST = 0.012, Holm 0.024) and for Stack-LR ([−0.0044, +0.0067], p = 0.0068, Holm 0.020). It fails for A-average: 90% CI [−0.0115, +0.0092], p_TOST = 0.080, because the per-seed F1 differences have s.d. 0.0151. The manuscript can therefore say that OTRG is statistically equivalent to the constant gate and to stacking within the stated margins, and equivalent to equal averaging in PR-AUC. The F1 equivalence with equal averaging is not demonstrated once seed variance is included. That is a failure to show equivalence, not evidence of a difference: the point estimate is −0.0010.

**Graph-view effects (H2).** For LGBM-AF+G versus LGBM-AF, the F1 gain of +0.0190 is the most robust result in the study. It is significant under every scheme: comm CI [+0.0118, +0.0279]; two-stage CI [+0.0083, +0.0319] with p = 0.0004 and family Holm ≤ 0.0072; positive in 5 of 5 seeds. The PR-AUC difference of +0.0004 is null everywhere (two-stage p = 0.80). The real-versus-shuffled-view PR-AUC gap is +0.0054. Under `comm` it has p = 0.0020 and family Holm 0.026. Under `step` it is p = 0.032, and under the two-stage bootstrap p = 0.0032 with family Holm 0.054. It is positive in 5 of 5 seeds (s.d. 0.00064). So it is borderline after full correction once seeds are included, and it should not be described as firmly significant. The corresponding F1 gap of +0.0072 does not survive the two-stage bootstrap (p = 0.39). In the secondary family, the shuffled view still raises F1 over node-only by +0.0118 (comm p = 0.0004, Holm 0.0048), as the reviewer noted. Against the new degree-preserving control (LGBM-AF+Gdp), the real view gains +0.0037 PR-AUC, with comm p = 0.0080, family Holm 0.096 and two-stage family Holm 0.174. It gains +0.0111 F1, with comm family Holm 0.011, two-stage p = 0.0092 and two-stage family Holm 0.147. Both gains are positive in 5 of 5 seeds. **After family-wide correction with seed variance included, there is no significant evidence that true topology beats a degree-preserving rewiring on either metric.** Before seeds are accounted for, the F1 gain over the rewiring is significant.

**A-dp-graph-gate versus OTRG.** Every number in this row is the mirror image of OTRG versus LGBM-AF. Its test predictions agree with LGBM-AF to within 1e-4 in absolute value for seeds 42 and 1 (I checked them directly). The gate fitted with the degree-preserving graph expert therefore collapses to the node-only expert. The author of that method should confirm that this is intended, and should not interpret the row as a separate result.

**Large gaps.** OTRG versus GCN, GraphSAGE, Mowst, LR, LGBM-LF and RF-Weber remain significant under every scheme. All raw p are at the B = 2000 floor of 0.001, and the cross-metric Holm values are ≤ 0.032. OTRG versus RF in PR-AUC (+0.0083) weakens from p = 0.001 (txn) to 0.004 (comm) and 0.049 (step), with cross-metric Holm 0.060. OTRG versus XGB in PR-AUC remains null (comm p = 0.10).

## Verdict

Criteria (b) to (e) are resolved. Seeds now enter the analysis, B is 5000 for the key family, p-values are reported with their attainable floors, Holm correction is applied across the comparison-by-metric family, and the step-level Wilcoxon is demoted to a descriptive role. Criterion (a) is resolved only approximately, because the requested component clusters do not exist at a usable granularity in Elliptic. Every test step is one component. The cluster bootstrap therefore uses Louvain communities, which are cut by roughly 5–12% of edges, and is checked against a 15-cluster step-level bootstrap. In substance, the two OTRG "wins" over the node-only expert do not survive full correction once training-seed variance is included. The negative finding (OTRG equivalent to a constant gate and to stacking) is now backed by formal equivalence tests. The only graph-view effect robust to everything is the F1 gain of LGBM-AF+G over LGBM-AF, and a degree-randomised view reproduces part of that gain.
